## 1. Introduction

### Section goal

Explain why the problem needs a connected ecosystem, who it is for, and what NovaGo deliberately does and does not attempt to replace.

### Verified concept source

- The course defines a super-app as a one-stop ecosystem of multiple integrated services within one interface.
- The course asks for a scope broad enough to support distinct services, but narrow enough for those services to feel like one system.
- The five original pitches addressed complementary concerns:
  - **Settly** — arrival checklist, maps, transport, and local-app setup.
  - **EZPAY** — payment guidance, accepted-method information, setup help, and payment-failure alternatives.
  - **BorrowNest** — trusted short-term borrowing with identity, handover, and condition records.
  - **NUS Nexus** — campus information, marketplace, packages, lost-and-found, and mutual help.
  - **UniFlow** — academic planning, campus services, growth, and collaboration.
- The integration proposal connected these ideas through one profile, one task centre, a shared campus location layer, and cross-service next steps.

### Proposed portfolio copy

> Starting university in a new country is not a single task. A student may need to understand a deadline, find an unfamiliar building, work out how to pay, source a temporary household item, and ask a “basic” question—all within the same day. The team identified these moments as one connected onboarding journey rather than five unrelated features.
>
> NovaGo is our concept for a focused campus super-app that helps NUS newcomers move from arrival to greater independence. Its value is not simply placing services on one screen; it is using the student’s current task, location, and stage of arrival to connect the right information and next action.

### Recommended content cards

- **Audience:** International newcomers, exchange students, and short-stay students at NUS.
- **Design challenge:** Turn fragmented arrival information into a clear, actionable first-week journey.
- **Scope:** Onboarding and early campus life, from arrival through the first semester.
- **Principle:** Connect distinct services through shared tasks, context, and progress.

### Boundary statement

> NovaGo is designed as an information, guidance, and coordination layer. The current concept does not claim to process real payments, replace Canvas or NUS administrative systems, hold deposits, or resolve marketplace disputes.

### Proposed portfolio copy

> We began with five team proposals and used them to define the assumptions that future research needs to test. Rather than treating feature ideas as findings, we reframed them as questions about three parts of the newcomer experience:
>
> 1. **Arrival and orientation:** Can a student turn unfamiliar places, terms, and deadlines into a first-week action path?
> 2. **Information and learning:** Can they judge which information is current, credible, and relevant to the task at hand?
> 3. **Trust and independence:** Can they ask for help and interact with campus peers without unnecessary uncertainty or social pressure?

These three themes come from the team’s **illustrative affinity exercise**, not from participant data.

### Research questions to use

- What are the highest-friction tasks during a newcomer’s first two weeks at NUS?
- Where do students currently look for answers, and how do they judge trustworthiness?
- Which tasks genuinely benefit from being connected, and which should remain a link to an existing service?
- What information would students be comfortable sharing for personalisation?
- What makes payment guidance, peer exchange, and community advice feel safe enough to use?

## 3. Ideation

### Verified concept source

The project began with five independently ownable ideas and then narrowed them into one newcomer journey. This directly supports the course requirement for a cohesive ecosystem with discrete services.

### Proposed section heading

> **Five starting points. One newcomer journey.**

### Proposed portfolio copy

> Our ideation process did not begin with a blank feature list. Each team member brought a different view of early campus life: settling in, paying with confidence, borrowing temporary items, accessing campus essentials, and transitioning into study and collaboration.
>
> We used the newcomer’s journey as the organising logic. A first-week plan can lead to a route, a route can reveal accepted payment methods, a checklist can suggest an item to borrow, and a course task can connect directly to its location or relevant community answer. This cross-service flow is what makes NovaGo a super-app concept rather than a folder of shortcuts.

### Idea-to-NovaGo mapping

| Starting concept | Contribution retained | NovaGo expression |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Settly | Arrival checklist, campus places, transport, local setup | First-week journey + **Smart Commute** |
| EZPAY | Payment-method guidance and failure alternatives | **Shared Payment** / payment guidance |
| BorrowNest | Trusted short-term access to useful items | **Campus Market** |
| NUS Nexus | Campus information and mutual help | **Campus Forum** + campus essentials |
| UniFlow | Academic planning and collaboration | **Academic Hub** |

The names **Smart Commute**, **Shared Payment**, **Campus Market**, and **Campus Forum** are verified in the existing poster. Their detailed interaction designs are not yet documented.

### Design principles supported by the sources

- **Synergy over feature quantity:** each service should hand off to a meaningful next step.
- **A narrow first release:** prioritise early-campus tasks instead of promising a complete university platform.
- **Distinct ownership:** each service needs a clear user flow that can be designed independently.
- **Feasible integration:** use guidance, simulated data, and deep links where official access or regulation would be required.
- **Task-first navigation:** organise the experience around what the student needs to complete, not five equally weighted icons.

### Proposed “How might we” statement

> **How might we help a student who is new to NUS understand and complete essential campus tasks without repeatedly searching across disconnected sources?**

### Existing visual direction

- Primary navy: `#203B88`

- Soft sky: `#DDF3FF`
- Soft mint: `#E3F8EA`
- Off-white background: `#F8FCFC`
- Rounded cards, generous spacing, simple line icons, and calm supportive language.
- Existing visual message: NovaGo should feel safe, gentle, and confidence-building rather than dense or administrative.

### Proposed portfolio copy

> The current visual direction translates the idea of a “gentler first week” into a calm, approachable system: deep navy for clarity, green for progress, soft contextual cards, and plain-language prompts. The next prototyping stage should test the experience as a connected task flow rather than build five isolated feature demos.

### Proposed core prototype flow

> Arrival profile → personalised first-week dashboard → contextual task card → relevant service → completion record / next step

### Proposed screen set

1. **Arrival setup:** exchange status, arrival date, residence, and optional preferences.
2. **First-week dashboard:** prioritised tasks, progress, and timely reminders.
3. **Academic Hub:** terms, forms, fees, deadlines, and direct links to official systems.
4. **Smart Commute:** destination, walking time, route mode, and timely departure guidance.
5. **Payment guidance:** accepted methods, setup information, recipient/total confirmation patterns, and fallback options.
6. **Campus Market:** useful nearby items, student-verification cues, public handover locations, and condition records.
7. **Campus Forum:** task-based questions, source/freshness cues, and optional low-pressure identity controls.

All seven screens are **proposed**, not evidence of a completed prototype.

### Honest section title for the current site

> **Evaluation Plan**

### Proposed portfolio copy

> Evaluation is the next step in the NovaGo process. The first study should test whether newcomers understand the task-first structure, whether cross-service handoffs reduce confusion, and whether trust cues are sufficient in payment, market, and forum scenarios. Until sessions are completed, this section records the test plan rather than claiming validated outcomes.

### Proposed usability tasks

- Find the next required onboarding action and identify its official source.
- Plan a route to an unfamiliar campus location and decide when to leave.
- Check which payment method can be used in a given campus scenario and find a fallback.
- Find a short-term item, assess whether the listing feels trustworthy, and choose a safe handover location.
- Ask or locate an answer to a newcomer question while controlling identity visibility.

### Proposed measures

- Task completion and critical errors.
- Time to identify the correct next action.
- Wrong turns between services.
- Confidence before and after each task.
- Ability to identify official versus peer-provided information.
- Perceived trust and clarity for payment, market, and forum interactions.
- Qualitative comments tied to participant IDs and task context.

## 6. Final Prototype

### Evidence status

No interactive final prototype is currently documented in the workspace. The NovaGo poster is a completed communication artefact, but it is not evidence of a functioning final prototype.

### Safe title for the current site

> **Final Concept Direction**

Use **“Final Prototype”** only after a real prototype link, screen set, or recorded walkthrough is available.

### Proposed portfolio copy

> NovaGo’s final concept direction brings five services into one newcomer-centred journey. Academic Hub clarifies what to do next; Smart Commute supports timely campus movement; payment guidance reduces uncertainty at the point of action; Campus Market supports access to useful items from campus peers; and Campus Forum creates a lower-pressure path to relevant help.
>
> The connecting layer is the product’s most important idea: a shared profile, prioritised tasks, campus context, and visible progress should allow one service to lead naturally to the next. The final interactive prototype should demonstrate this continuity from arrival setup through the completion of a real first-week task.

### What the eventual final prototype must demonstrate

- One coherent onboarding-to-action flow.
- At least one meaningful handoff between two services.
- Clear distinction between official information and peer advice.
- Trust and privacy cues where identity, payment, or exchange is involved.
- A completion state that gives the student a clear next step.
- Honest treatment of external systems through deep links or simulated data.

### Suggested closing copy

> **Arrive curious. Leave confident.**  
> NovaGo brings the first week together—safely, simply, and gently.
